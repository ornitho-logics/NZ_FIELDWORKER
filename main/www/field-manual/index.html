1. How to use this manual

1. How to use this manual

If you have time, please browse this manual before going to the field. You may also consult it from the FIELDWORKER app on your phone in the field if you have data coverage. Start with Section 2 if you are unsure which data records an observation/encounter needs. Read the short procedure first; use its supporting table when entering the data.

The procedures apply to our Banded Dotterel / Pohowera work throughout New Zealand. Use BADO for the species and the approved code for your study site. CR means Cass River; it is an example here, not the code for every site. Site-specific nest-ID schemes, banding patterns and geolocator allocations below are labelled examples or project instructions, not permission to apply them at another site. Check your team's approved scheme and app configuration with the field lead. Wrybill nests may also be found and photographed: use WRYB and the team's WR nest IDs. Keep WRYB records separate from BADO in your notes. Other species and site codes in the app support wider project work; they are not additional procedures covered by this manual.

Here, a geolocator/tag is a tracking unit fitted to a bird, not the Garmin GPS or camera. Section 8 explains geolocator deployment, retrieval and tag records.

Record what happened

Give each bird its own record, even when siblings have the same colour band. A bird only seen goes in the resighting form; a bird caught and handled goes in the capture form. Do not add a second sighting simply because the chick was captured. A genuine observation before handling can be recorded separately.

Hiding-spot photographs are a specific exception to the single record: each photographed chick needs its own H-class resighting as well as any capture record (e.g., if the chick was unbanded when the hiding spot photo was taken, there should be a capture record to describe the banding plus a resighting record to describe the hiding spot photo. A hiding spot photo of a banded chick requires only the resighting record). A tent photograph stays with the capture. Section 7 explains the separation.

Field record Database table Number of rows
Nest or scrape visit NESTS One new row per visit.
Egg measurements EGGS One row per egg measured on that visit.
Bird caught or recaptured CAPTURES One row per individual per handling event.
Bird observed but not handled RESIGHTINGS One row per individual per observation.
Chick photographed in its hiding place RESIGHTINGS, rclass = H One row per photographed chick.

A row is one line in the entry form. Two banded chicks seen together need two chick rows, not one row with “two chicks” in the comments.

Required, conditional, optional or blank?

Wording in this manual Meaning
Required Complete this field for the stated event.
Conditional Complete it when the stated condition applies.
Optional Enter it if obtained; do not invent it.
Leave blank / not applicable The field does not apply to that event. Do not fill it with zero or NA as a substitute.

Zero is a real count or an explicit “no” in a 0/1 field. An unknown count is not zero. X in a leg-mark field means an observed empty position; ? means marks were seen but not confidently read. These are different observations.

Follow the field lead's directions, permits and welfare limits. This manual connects fieldwork to the database; practical training still governs trapping, handling, measuring, sampling and photographic technique.

2. What have I encountered?

2. What have I encountered?

An encounter may need several records. Work through every applicable branch: finding a nest, floating eggs and catching a parent are separate things to record, even when they happen on one visit.

Master decision tree

What happened during the encounter? Follow every applicable branch.
├─ Nest or scrape checked → one NESTS visit [4, 9]
│  ├─ Eggs measured/floated → one EGGS row per egg [4]
│  └─ Clutch photographed → photo fields on that NESTS visit [7]
├─ Bird only seen → one RESIGHTINGS row per bird [5]
│  └─ Family present → record observed parent(s) and chick(s), shared waypoint [5]
├─ Bird caught/handled → one CAPTURES row per bird [6]
│  ├─ Tent photograph → photo fields on that capture [7]
│  └─ Geolocator/tag fitted/removed/retained → tag fields on that capture [8]
└─ Chick photographed hiding → separate H-class RESIGHTINGS row per chick [7]

Equivalent text key

  1. Did you check a nest or scrape? Yes: enter a nest visit, then go to 2. No: go to 2 without a nest visit.
  2. Did you measure eggs? Yes: add one egg row per egg. No: do not create egg readings. In either case, go to 3.
  3. Was a bird caught and handled? Yes: enter its capture; do not manufacture a duplicate sighting. No, it was only seen: enter its resighting. Then go to 4.
  4. Were other family members seen? Yes: record each observed parent and chick, using the family waypoint rules. No: do not add absent birds. Go to 5.
  5. Were photographs or videos taken? Yes: use the appropriate event's photo fields; hiding photographs always need separate H rows. No: leave unrelated media fields blank. Go to 6.
  6. Was there a geolocator/tag action, hatch, failure or closure? Yes: also follow Section 8 or 9. No: check the records identified above before leaving.

Which nest or brood ID?

  1. Does the bird belong to a known nest? Yes: retain that positive nest ID, including after the brood leaves the nest. No: go to 2.
  2. Is this a mobile brood whose nest and eggs were never found? Yes: use its assigned negative brood ID. No: do not assign a negative ID merely because you are unsure which known nest the bird belongs to. Go to 3.
  3. Is an adult being captured when clearly unrelated to any nest or brood? Yes: leave nest_id blank and enter the capture's gps_id/point. No: follow the event-specific association rules; do not invent a placeholder ID.

The rare discovery of newly hatched chicks inside an obvious nest establishes a positive nest origin. Record its F discovery followed by H as described in Section 4. Finding chicks outside a nest does not establish that origin.

Some later chick resightings, and a narrowly defined negative-brood family encounter, can leave the ID blank. Use the checks in Section 5; a blank field is not a negative brood ID and must not be filled with a placeholder.

Which resighting class?

  1. Chick photographed in its hiding place? Yes: H. No: go to 2.
  2. Failed capture attempt? Yes: C. No: go to 3.
  3. Video session? Yes: V. No: go to 4.
  4. Photography session? Yes: P. No: R for a regular sighting.

The same letter can have different meanings in different columns:

Entry Meaning
age = C Chick.
rclass = C Failed capture attempt; the bird was not handled.
capture_status = C Recapture in which the band combination changed.
tag_action = R Tag retrieved, not a regular resighting.
3. Before leaving the field house

3. Before leaving the field house

Check the current To-Do PDF and agree the day's work with the field lead. Take the Parent–Nest summary, allocated band combinations and the equipment needed for the visits you have been assigned. Read any capture timing notes before planning to trap a parent.

Equipment and time settings

  • Charge the Garmin GPS and camera; check their unit IDs against your notes.
  • Take field forms, spare batteries/cards, rings, bands, flags, measuring equipment and the labelled sample materials needed for the day's work.
  • Use local New Zealand date and 24-hour time. Check the camera and GPS clocks together, including the correct daylight-saving setting.
  • Keep the Garmin switched on in the field. Do not delete or manually rename its points. Check recording settings against the app's GPS help if needed.

Collect a waypoint with your Garmin GPS

At a new nest, brood encounter, capture location, regular resighting, or hiding-photo location, save a waypoint and immediately write down the Garmin unit ID and point number. Point 214 on GPS 4 is not the same record as point 214 on GPS 5.

For a family encounter, use the same waypoint for every family member recorded on that occasion. This includes a situation in which the chick(s) were captures and the parent(s) were only resightings – simultaneous events that are entered into two different tables (CAPTURES and RESIGHTINGS). Collect a new point for a later encounter after the family moves; do not reuse yesterday's point because the brood ID is unchanged. Enter nest_id whenever it is known. Use the Parent–Nest summary on the To-Do PDF to check the family link; otherwise follow the blank-ID conditions in Section 5. Absence from a To-Do table does not by itself permit a blank ID.

Quick GPS key

  1. Is this the first nest/scrape visit? Yes: enter its discovery gps_id/point. No, a later visit to that stationary nest: leave both nest GPS fields blank. For a bird encounter, go to 2.
  2. Is it a brood, H photograph, blank-ID family event, age-C status R or C capture or adult capture with chicks? Yes: use the encounter's gps_id/point as specified above. No: go to 3.
  3. Does the permitted event use a positive nest's location? Yes: follow its row in the table. No: the direct-location branch needs both GPS fields.

Upload the GPS before entering biological records at the field house. The entered gps_id/point must exist among the uploaded points. The biological forms use gps_id and gps_point; do not type latitude and longitude into those fields.

4. Nests, scrapes and eggs

4. Nests, scrapes and eggs

A new nest or scrape

Keep the first visit short. Confirm the species, collect a waypoint with your Garmin GPS and assign the next unused nest ID under the team's naming system (see below). Write down the eggs, chicks and attending bird actually seen. Photograph and process the nest only as directed by the visit plan and welfare conditions.

For example, Cass River BADO IDs use an A, B or C plot prefix and the assigned two-digit observer/GPS code plus two-digit running tally, for example B0203. Do not restart your tally in another plot: B0203 and A0203 must not be assigned as different discoveries using the same code/tally. Check the current register. WRYB nests use the separate WR naming system.

New discovery or later visit?

  1. Does the scrape/nest already have an ID? Yes: keep it, choose today's state from the evidence using Section 9, and go to 4. No: record the discovery waypoint and go to 2.
  2. Are eggs present at the new nest? Yes: assign its ID and enter the opening F visit, then go to 4. No: go to 3.
  3. Is this a scrape with neither eggs nor chicks? Yes: assign its ID and enter S with zero eggs. No: were newly hatched chicks found inside an obvious nest? If yes, use the rare discovery procedure below. If no, use the brood guidance in Section 5; do not invent a nest origin.
  4. Were eggs measured or floated? Yes: add an egg row per egg measured. No: do not create readings. Go to 5.
  5. Are chicks present, or is fate uncertain? Yes: also use Section 9. No: complete the active visit and its planned follow-up.

A known scrape can later have its single F discovery when eggs are found; retain its ID and the original scrape waypoint.

Rare case: the nest is first found with newly hatched chicks inside

Banded Dotterel chicks are precocial and nidifugous: they can move soon after hatching and leave the nest shortly afterwards. Finding them still inside an obvious nest immediately after hatch is therefore very rare.

If that is what you find, assign a positive nest ID and collect the nest waypoint. Enter two NESTS events: F to record discovery, followed immediately by H, with the H time at least one minute after F. Explain that the nest was first found with newly hatched chicks inside it. This records discovery and hatch at the same encounter; it does not invent an earlier egg visit.

Event Required entries for this exception
F discovery New positive nest_id, actual discovery date/time, observer/species/site and both nest GPS fields. If no eggs remain, clutch_size = 0; do not invent eggs or egg measurements. Record the chicks actually seen.
Immediate H Same positive ID and encounter date; time_visit at least one minute after F. Record actual remaining eggs/chicks; with no eggs, clutch 0 and the observed positive brood count. Leave direct nest GPS blank because F establishes the point.
Individual birds Separate CAPTURES rows if handled or RESIGHTINGS rows if observed; family and photograph rules still apply.

When validation flags this observation: a first F row with zero eggs correctly triggers the usual safeguard. Recheck the entry. If chicks really were found inside an obvious nest immediately after hatch, keep the true counts and explain this rare circumstance in comments. For example: “Nest first found with newly hatched chicks still inside an obvious nest; no eggs remained. F discovery followed by H.”

If saving remains blocked, check the other fields and use the documented validation override in Section 12, retaining the explanation. This is an exceptional, commented observation—not a reason to weaken the inspector, invent eggs or add a programmed exception.

Chicks first encountered outside an obvious nest, with no previously known nest origin, form an unknown-origin brood: use a negative ID and no NESTS events. Do not make F/H rows merely because chicks look newly hatched. A brood already reliably linked to a known nest retains its positive ID, as in Section 5.

Measuring and floating eggs

Check the egg's number before each reading and return eggs promptly under the team's procedure. Do not float starred or pipping eggs. Record their hatch signs on the nest visit instead; if an egg row is needed to explain omitted measurements, leave its float fields blank and explain why.

Clutch photographs belong on the nest visit, not in the egg table. Photographing two eggs without measuring them does not, by itself, require two EGGS rows.

5. Adults, chicks and broods

5. Adults, chicks and broods

When you find a family

Record the adult(s) and each chick you actually encounter. Read the marks you can see, note what the adults are doing and collect a waypoint with your Garmin GPS. Keep the family together in your notebook so the entries can be linked later.

Record both social parents when both are observed and identifiable. If only one is seen, record that bird; do not add the absent mate. An unbanded parent still needs a row, using X-X. Two banded chicks need two rows even if their single colour bands are the same.

Which family is it?

  1. Is the brood reliably linked to a known nest? Yes: retain that positive ID. No: go to 2.
  2. Was the nest never found? Yes: use the assigned negative brood ID. No, the origin is merely uncertain: check the parents against the Parent–Nest summary on the To-Do PDF; ask the field lead if the match remains unclear.

Keep the leading minus sign on a negative ID. It denotes a mobile brood of unknown origin, not a failed nest or an unidentified adult. Negative IDs belong only in CAPTURES and RESIGHTINGS, never NESTS or EGGS.

For an adult capture clearly unrelated to any nest or brood, leave nest_id blank and enter the capture's gps_id/point. This is not a way to record an unknown family association: a brood still needs its actual link or an eligible blank-ID route described below.

Give the family one shared waypoint

Every family record from this encounter must carry the same GPS unit and point number, with the same date and site. This applies across tables: if chicks were captured but the parents only resighted, their records still need the same waypoint. These entries are crucial when reconstructing a missing nest/brood link.

For example, two chick captures and one parent resighting at GPS 4 / point 214 all use gps_id = 4 and gps_point = 214. A point number alone, a point from another GPS, or yesterday's date cannot identify that shared encounter.

All records from this family encounter must agree on the nest or brood ID where one is entered. Different nonblank IDs at the same date, site and gps_id/point trigger a data-entry error, even when the chick's ID is also entered. Check the notebook and family identity; do not invent a second waypoint to make the entry pass. This does not prevent a chick's association changing between separate encounters, for example after brood mixing.

When does an observation confirm the parent–nest link?

When the To-Do PDF asks you to confirm a parent's nest association, record what that bird actually does at the nest or brood. Scraping (SC) can confirm the link after one clear, nest-linked observation. It does not require chicks to be present. Do not add chick rows unless chicks were actually encountered.

The check applies to each parent separately. Confirming one bird does not confirm its mate, so the family may still appear under Parents to resight for nest association.

These are codes for the form's behav field. Use the behaviour actually seen, not the code most likely to remove a To-Do entry. An adult alert nearby is AT; use AC only for an adult alert with chicks. If several behaviours were observed, enter the applicable codes separated by commas, for example AT, IN.

How the database chooses between possible parents

The database assesses males and females separately. It first identifies the bird, then weighs the evidence linking that bird to this particular nest or brood. A colour combination recognised from an earlier season establishes identity, not ownership of a current nest.

Evidence, strongest first How it affects the parent–nest link
A qualifying direct capture A nest-linked adult caught by TN, TB or HA, or with chicks recorded in caught_with, supplies direct association evidence. An eligible adult capture linked to a banded chick through the exact family encounter can also establish the link. Record the actual method/context; never choose a capture code to strengthen the result.
One confirming resighting One of IN, NM, SC, BW, BC, FC or AC above. This takes precedence over an alternative identity supported only by repeated other behaviour.
Three other matching resightings Three genuine, eligible nest-linked observations of the same identity, including the first. These are observation records, not the two separate dates per parent used for brood-loss evidence in Section 9. Do not split one observation or duplicate rows to reach three.
Insufficient or conflicting evidence The link remains pending if no identity reaches a qualifying level, or ambiguous if more than one identity shares the strongest level. The database does not choose a bird merely because it was seen nearby.

An ambiguous mark match also prevents that candidate from being treated as resolved, even when some of its other evidence appears strong. Obtain a better reading and refer the conflict; do not remove the original observation.

A mobile-mistnet capture (capture_method = MM) does not, by itself, prove which nest that adult belongs to. Obtain genuine matching follow-up sightings. For an MM follow-up, one confirming behaviour or three matching post-capture resightings can resolve the link. The To-Do follow-up can begin two calendar days after capture; this is separate from the 36-hour capture-disturbance rule in Section 8. A timing-blocked capture instruction can also appear as resighting work, so read the To-Do note rather than assuming every resighting row means uncertain parent identity.

Read as much of the mark as possible. Most fully colour-banded adults can be matched by their lower-leg combination; birds distinguished by an upper-leg spacer need that part read too. A verified metal ring can identify a bird when its observed colour marks are uncertain. Otherwise, ? anywhere in the four leg fields excludes that event from parent-association evidence; it is not a wildcard matching every possible colour. Keep the original observation. An X-X adult can be recorded as a current parent, but X-X cannot establish that it is the same unbanded individual at another nest or on another date.

Later capture/banding or convincing evidence of a different parent can change the selected identity. Older observations must not be copied forward as new sightings. Check the refreshed Parent–Nest summary and refer conflicting marks, sexes or dates rather than editing observations to make them agree.

The field requirement is a genuine shared family encounter: the same date, site and gps_id/point. The reviewed local matching rules enforce that shared encounter and reject conflicting family IDs. Check the actual field records if the Parent–Nest summary looks wrong; an automatic result is not permission to ignore a mistaken date, waypoint or identity.

A resolved parent is not a dead brood. A row can disappear from Parents to resight for nest association because the link is now established, while the brood remains on banding or flagging lists. The separate presumed-dead and juvenile-stage resolution checks are explained in Section 9.

An unbanded parent, or no parent seen

If an unbanded parent is tending chicks, record it as X-X: at minimum LL = X, LR = X. Add its actual behaviour, age, sex, observer, date, site, required ID and shared gps_id/point. The updated snapshot uses the latest qualifying chick encounter on or before the PDF's reference date, not simply the largest earlier brood count. Foraging alone / with presumed mate / in flock FA / FM / FF Counts towards the stronger non-brood evidence when properly identified and explicitly linked to the old brood, without positive brood or nesting/restart behaviour on that row.
Roosting / loafing / alert / fighting RS / LF / AT / FT Counts on the same basis as FA/FM/FF. General alert AT is not alert with chicks AC.
Other behaviour OB Counts on that same basis in the current calculation. Explain exactly what occurred in comments; OB is not a code for “brood gone”.
Preening or courtship only PR or CO Useful field observations, but do not count towards the repeated non-brood-loss threshold. They do not independently confirm nest association either.

An observation can serve different purposes. For example, three genuine AT resightings may establish a parent's nest association, but AT observations made before the brood's hatch evidence cannot establish subsequent brood loss. A row containing AT, BC is positive brood evidence, not negative evidence.

Route 1 — repeated non-brood observations of both parents

All the following conditions must be met for this route. A negative brood uses its discovery date, because its true hatch date may be unknown. For a positive nest, the starting date is the earliest recorded H visit or positive brood count; if neither exists, the first linked chick capture supplies hatch evidence. This is the database's evidence date, not a claim to know the exact hatching time.

Requirement What to obtain or check

Simply reaching 21 days, or failing to find the chicks for 21 days, is not sufficient for this route. The separate fixed-window rule in Route 3 starts only from day 36.

Route 2 — former parent(s) associated with another nest (renesting evidence)

Renesting means starting another nesting attempt. An associated scrape is enough: there is no need to wait for eggs. Record the new attempt under its own positive nest_id, then record the adult's actual association with it. One eligible SC observation can confirm the scraping parent's link; IN or NM can also confirm a link when those behaviours are genuinely observed. Do not add a behaviour merely to remove the old brood from the To-Do PDF.

Luke Eberhart-Hertel's project rule treats qualifying renesting as automatic presumed loss of the earlier dependent brood: BADO parents do not begin another attempt while still tending that brood. This route has no 21-day wait. Record any contrary chick evidence truthfully and refer an apparent contradiction to Luke Eberhart-Hertel. If the old brood has already resolved through reliable age-J evidence, a new attempt does not turn that developmental resolution into presumed death.

Route 3 — no live brood encounter during ages 21–35 days

Plan to locate broods again between ages 21 and 35 days, inclusive. From day 36, a brood with no qualifying live encounter anywhere in that window is presumed dead for planning. Its brood work and associated parent capture and resighting tasks are removed after refresh, along with its brood entry in the Parent–Nest summary. This rule does not require the two-parent observations in Route 1 or renesting evidence in Route 2.

Check How this rule works
Which age is used? For a positive nest, count from its recorded hatch evidence, not its egg-stage discovery. For a negative brood, use the existing operational age/discovery-date proxy: this is not a claim that discovery was its true hatch date. Without a usable age anchor, this rule cannot establish presumed loss.
When does the window end? Day 35 is still within the observation window. The first possible suppression date is day 36. All checks use the PDF's reference date and records available for that date.
What counts as an encounter? The reliably linked live chick or positive brood-tending evidence described below. Direct nest/brood IDs and approved unambiguous ring-history or family gps_id/point links count. An unidentified adult alone, an ambiguous link or a dead chick recovery alone does not count.
What if the brood was seen on day 25? One qualifying live encounter on any day from 21 through 35 prevents this particular trigger, even at later reference dates. This is a fixed window, not a rolling absence period. Routes 1 and 2 and juvenile-stage resolution still apply independently.
What if chicks are found again after day 35? Enter the actual encounter. A qualifying live encounter on day 36 or later prevents this presumed-dead result at the next refresh and restores any work still due under the normal task rules. Do not change the observation to fit the earlier expectation.

Nothing in this calculation proves that the chicks died. Do not enter a dead recovery, failure visit, zero brood count or invented unsuccessful search just to obtain the result. Upload genuine observations promptly, and ask for a refresh before relying on the dated work plan. A required positive-nest notA visit remains separate; negative broods never receive NESTS closure rows.

A later confirmed death does not erase an earlier live encounter within the window. If a resighting conflicts with a death recorded on the same or an earlier date, refer the identity and chronology for review rather than using it automatically to establish survival.

Live evidence can keep the brood open or bring it back

Before suppressing To-Do work, the database checks for any of the following dated on or after the candidate loss date, up to the reference date:

  • An age-C CAPTURES row with live capture_status F, R or C, reliably linked to the old brood.
  • An age-C RESIGHTINGS row reliably linked to the old brood.
  • A reliably established former parent's BC, FC or AC resighting explicitly linked to the old brood, directly or through an approved unambiguous link.

Any such record prevents the presumed-dead result at that refresh. Brood work can reappear where it still meets its normal age, timing and completion rules. It does not guarantee that every previous To-Do entry returns; for example, the hiding-photo display window may already have expired.

For Route 3, the candidate loss date is day 36. The same qualifying live evidence also prevents that route when dated within days 21–35. A late upload of a genuine day-25 encounter therefore corrects the result after refresh; do not alter its field date to the upload date.

An approved unambiguous inferred link counts too; a blank raw nest_id does not mean that the event has no family association. Enter the known ID whenever available. Otherwise follow Section 5, retaining the shared date, site and gps_id/point. Conflicting explicit IDs, ambiguous parents or uncertain marks without a verified known ring cannot supply an inferred association. Do not change a truthful date or waypoint to make an event count.

A capture with status D is a dead recovery, not evidence of survival. An unknown capture status is not reliable live evidence either. One dead chick does not establish that the entire brood died: a sibling may remain alive. Record the recovered bird as its own CAPTURES row and continue recording any living siblings and tending parents actually encountered.

One origin-linked juvenile resolves chick-stage work

Record the bird's actual age. An engraved flag identifies a bird; it does not by itself establish age J. One genuinely age-J capture or resighting that can be reliably traced to its original brood resolves that brood's chick-stage work. Under Luke Eberhart-Hertel's project rule, siblings develop at the same rate and are presumed to have reached J too. Do not wait for a separate J record for each sibling simply to clear the brood's To-Do work.

A readable flag may be traced through the capture that linked it to a metal ring, then back to the original chick capture. Later brood mixing does not replace that original origin. Contradictory ring/flag identities or an ambiguous original brood need review; they must not resolve whichever ID happens to be entered on the new row.

Field decision key — what evidence should I collect next?

  1. Has a genuinely age-J bird been reliably linked to its original brood? Yes: record that individual, including a dead recovery if that is what was found, and check juvenile-stage resolution after refresh. No: go to 2.
  2. Have chicks or chick-tending parents been seen? Yes: record the actual family, each bird separately, with shared date, site and gps_id/point; do not call the brood dead. No: go to 3.
  3. Are the former parents reliably identified? No: collect mark and actual association behaviour using Section 5; unresolved identity cannot supply the two-parent loss inference. Yes: go to 4.
  4. Is a former parent genuinely associated with a new attempt, including a scrape? Yes: record that nest under its own ID and the actual parent behaviour; check Route 2 and report contrary chick evidence. No: go to 5.
  5. Have both former parents been observed without brood-tending on two separate dates each? No: plan suitable repeat observations, without disturbance or invented records. Yes: check all Route 1 conditions, including the 21-day gate, explicit old brood links and BW limitation.
  6. Has the brood reached day 36 without any qualifying live encounter at ages 21–35? Yes: Route 3 can suppress its work after refresh. Record any later live encounter truthfully so eligible work can return. A day-25 encounter prevents this route; simply seeing an adult alone does not.
  7. Is the brood absent from a newly refreshed PDF? Check the reason with the designated operator. Resolved parent association, normal completion, expiry, presumed brood loss and juvenile-stage resolution are different reasons for disappearance. Missing work or contrary survival evidence still needs a handover.

For event tables and examples, see Sections 13 and 14. No fieldworker needs to alter a status table or manually “tick off” a brood: enter genuine evidence, then have the designated operator refresh and review the dated work plan.

Appendix B. Field and submission checklists

Appendix B. Field and submission checklists

Before leaving the field house

  • Current To-Do PDF, reference date and team allocation checked.
  • Garmin/camera charged, IDs and local NZ clocks checked.
  • Notebook, rings/bands/flags, measurement and sample equipment ready.
  • Capture and GEO timing/eligibility checked before planning a trap.

Before leaving the encounter

  • Each bird and egg measurement has its own notebook line.
  • Actual nest/brood ID retained; negative minus sign intact.
  • Garmin waypoint collected where needed; unit ID and point written down.
  • Family date, site and gps_id/point shared across sightings and captures.
  • Adult behaviour recorded as observed; AT distinguished from AC and IN/NM/SC linked to the actual nest concerned.
  • All observed parents recorded; no absent parent invented.
  • Unbanded tending parent recorded X-X; unreadable resighting marks use ?.
  • Incoming/release capture marks read directly; no ? in any leg field.
  • Each photo sequence identifies subject/ring, camera and full endpoints.
  • Tent and hiding sequences distinct; sample/tag labels checked.

Before saving

  • Field date/time and observer checked against prefilled defaults.
  • GPS uploaded; both numbers valid and match the encounter.
  • First NESTS visit has GPS; later visits do not repeat it.
  • Rare chicks-inside-nest discovery uses positive ID, F then H at least one minute later, with true counts; outside unknown-origin brood has no NESTS rows.
  • If the rare correct zero-egg F triggers validation, the observation was rechecked and explained in comments; any override is documented, not a request to weaken the inspector.
  • Egg numbers/counts, float blanks versus zero and actual times checked.
  • Genuine chick captures saved before parent-only/H entries that use them.
  • Parent and chick resightings from one encounter submitted together.
  • No extra chick sightings invented alongside captures.
  • Any blank ordinary-family or later-capture ID satisfies its specific conditions; the exception does not extend to a new H row.
  • Conflicting family identities referred, not silently selected.
  • Missing-parent exception has a specific truthful comment, if needed.
  • Age-C status R or C captures have both GPS fields even with a positive ID.
  • Every new H row has an explicit valid positive/negative nest_id, ring, leg observations, gps_id/point and camera/range.
  • Class P or V media fields complete; ordinary ring optionality not applied to H.
  • Tag action, actual old/new IDs and adult-only fields checked.
  • Capture status agrees with before/after combination; samples use own codes.
  • A D recovery has actual incoming marks and blank release fields/time, whether age C or J; no flag or sighting invented.
  • Unrelated adult captures have blank nest_id and gps_id/point; no placeholder IDs entered. Negative IDs appear only in bird tables.
  • Validation reviewed; nov/pk not edited; any justified override documented.

Before finishing the day

  • Saved rows match notebook counts; no duplicate submissions.
  • Original media copied and backup checked without renaming files.
  • Updated To-Do PDF checked, including reference date and refresh status.
  • Missing parent/brood work checked for association resolution, completion, expiry, presumed loss or juvenile-stage resolution—not assumed to mean chicks died.
  • Broods sought again during ages 21–35; any later live encounter or late-uploaded window encounter handed over for a new refresh, without changing its field date.
  • A confirmed origin-linked J record, including D, checked for brood-stage resolution; known sibling deaths retained and positive notA work still checked.
  • Former parents, separate-date non-brood observations, new nesting attempts and any contrary chick evidence handed over with their correct IDs; Section 9.
  • Hiding photographs planned before actual age seven days, not discovery age.
  • Missing photographs checked per ring; expiry not mistaken for completion.
  • Unresolved family links, validation discrepancies and unfinished work handed over.
  • pP, P or D fate checks remain in the handover until final notA, even if absent from the To-Do PDF.
