Banded Dotterel / Pohowera — Core Team Field Guide

Core Team Field Guide · Edition 1 · 3 November 2026

For experienced team members working under the approved permits, training, and site instructions. This guide summarises the Full Protocol Manual. For complete requirements and exceptions, follow the linked procedures. If an entry or situation is unclear, keep the record truthful and consult the linked procedure or Luke Eberhart-Hertel.

1. Before leaving

  • Check the To-Do PDF's reference date, team allocation, and unfinished work from the previous day. A missing task does not prove completion.
  • Check capture restrictions before planning a trap. Carry the allocated rings, bands, and flags; check Garmin/camera IDs, batteries, and NZ clocks.
  • Use BADO and your approved site code. Cass River nest-ID, marking, and geolocator instructions are not automatically applicable at another site.

2. Record the encounter, not the expected result

What happened? Record
Nest or scrape visited One NESTS row per actual visit.
Eggs measured One EGGS row per egg measured.
Bird handled One CAPTURES row per bird per handling event.
Bird observed One RESIGHTINGS row per bird per observation.
Chick photographed hiding A separate RESIGHTINGS row per chick, rclass = H, in addition to any handling record.

Do not invent a sighting because a bird was captured. A genuine observation before handling may be recorded separately. Siblings with the same colour band still need separate records.

Comments supplement events; they do not replace them. The To-Do algorithm does not read free-text comments as substitutes for the required event records. An observation entered only in comments will therefore be overlooked as evidence for the corresponding task decision. Missing event records can leave parent associations unresolved or make the work plan incomplete or misleading.

For example, writing a bird's identity in NESTS.comments is not a resighting. Keep the nest visit in NESTS and enter the observed bird separately in RESIGHTINGS, with its actual marks, sex, behaviour, nest ID, and other required observation details. Use behav = IN only if it was actually incubating. bird_inc records attendance/sex, not individual identity, and does not replace the resighting either. The same principle applies to any event with its own required record: enter it in the correct form, not only in another row's comments.

Keep the correct ID. A known nest's positive ID stays with its mobile brood. A negative ID means the nest/origin was never found: retain the minus sign and use it only in bird tables, never NESTS or EGGS. Uncertain identity does not justify inventing a negative ID. Clearly unrelated adult captures use a blank nest_id and both GPS fields; never enter NO_NEST.

Keep blanks meaningful. Zero is an observed count or an applicable “no,” not unknown. X means an observed empty band position; ? means unreadable marks in resightings, not an empty position. Capture leg fields must not contain ?.

3. Keep family records linked

Collect a waypoint at the encounter and write down both gps_id and gps_point. Parent and chick records from that family encounter must agree on the date, site, and shared GPS pair. Use a new waypoint when the family is encountered later elsewhere.

  • First nest visit: enter GPS. Later stationary nest visits: leave both GPS fields blank.
  • Negative-ID bird records, every new H-photo row, and age-C recaptures with status R or C require both GPS fields, even when a positive ID is known.
  • Adult captures with chicks require the GPS pair and the actual caught_with entry. First chick captures require the actual positive or negative ID.
  • Other positive-nest events have specific GPS/blank-field exceptions: use the GPS requirements, not a blanket rule to fill every field.

Record each parent actually observed, including an unbanded parent as X-X (LL = X, LR = X). Do not invent an absent mate. Use the behaviour seen: AT is not AC. BC, FC, and AC describe chick-tending; IN, NM, SC, and BW can also confirm a nest link, but BW alone does not establish chicks seen. Other eligible behaviour needs three matching observations to establish the individual's link. Never split or duplicate one observation to reach three. Qualifying nest-linked TN, TB, or HA captures can establish association directly; record the real method and context, not a code chosen to strengthen the result.

MM capture alone does not prove parentage. Earlier eligible evidence can count for a reliably identified bird; X-X is not a unique identity across dates. A unique eligible first-banding capture can inherit same-day, same-sex, same-nest confirming X-X evidence under the approved targeting assumption. Refer competing identities or changed nest links rather than guessing.

Submission order: upload GPS first. If chicks were handled, save their genuine captures before parent-only resightings or H rows that rely on them. Submit parent and chick resightings from one encounter together. A prior parent capture does not replace the accompanying-parent requirement for new chick resightings. For a genuine no-parent encounter, keep the chick observations, waypoint, and specific explanation; follow the exception procedure rather than fabricating a parent.

4. Nest visits and eggs

  • A bird observed at the nest needs its own resighting record; its identity in the nest-visit comments alone will not supply To-Do association evidence.
  • New empty scrape: S, zero clutch. New nest with eggs: F. Keep the same ID for subsequent visits and record actual counts, times, and evidence.
  • Rare first discovery of newly hatched chicks inside an obvious nest: positive ID, F then H at least one minute later, truthful counts, and an explanation. Chicks outside a never-found nest use a negative brood ID, not invented nest visits. Consult the rare-discovery procedure if validation blocks.
  • clutch_size means eggs remaining; brood_size means chicks observed. hatch_state describes the eggs: N no sign, S star, C crack, CC multiple/large cracks. Do not return H to I because an egg remains.
  • Measure only appropriate eggs; do not float starred or pipping eggs. Keep actual measurement times. Floating fields for submerged eggs must not be filled as though they were surface measurements.
  • The first H with no eggs remaining and chicks present is the final hatch visit. Later mobile-brood encounters go in bird tables. Final notA closure remains separate.

5. Handling, marking, and photographs

Read the ring and actual incoming marks before making changes. Record actual release marks separately: F first marking, R unchanged recapture, C changed combination, D dead recovery. D uses the recovered incoming marks, with release marks and release time blank. Do not manufacture a release. Complete the age/status-specific measurements, samples, and applicable flags in the capture requirements.

Follow the flagging To-Do and welfare assessment: follow-up begins from day 21, but do not flag below 22 g or 27 mm tarsus. Those thresholds do not replace the bander's judgement. Flagging an already banded chick changes its combination: status C. Use only the allocated marking scheme.

Hiding photograph: aim before actual age seven days. Each photographed chick needs its own age-C, sex-U, class-H resighting with an explicit valid positive/negative ID, known metal ring, actual leg observations, both GPS fields, camera ID, and full photo endpoints. Save necessary banding records first. Keep real attending-parent observations with the submission.

A tent photograph belongs to the capture; a hiding photograph belongs to H. Do not substitute the capture photo flag or tent sequence for the H record. Keep original filenames and distinguish individuals by metal ring. The photo task expiring on day seven does not mean it was completed; negative-brood discovery age is not necessarily biological age.

6. Capture and geolocator restrictions

Both the seven-day and 36-hour restrictions still apply to pair completion. The seven-day calculation follows final-egg laying evidence, not discovery or the date completion was confirmed. The 36-hour restriction follows relevant adult capture. The dated PDF assumes 08:00 on its reference date; it is not a live countdown. Follow the full timing procedure and welfare assessment.

  • A resighting-only row is not permission to capture or deploy a tag.
  • “Band” does not mean “tag.” An already-tagged possible parent needs its association checked, not another tag.
  • Follow conditional tagging notes literally: “tag any, but only if one is confirmed X-X” requires confirmation of the unbanded bird and its association. An already-banded unknown mate does not satisfy that initial-deployment condition.
  • The current 2026 Cass River late-wave rule uses eligible B/C positive nests with clutch completion after 9 October, not initiation. Apply only the approved site allocation and same-nest pair-completion rules.

Record actual adult tag action, before/after tag IDs, and spacer information. Chick/juvenile tag fields stay blank. Consult the complete geolocator and timing instructions before capture or any exceptional deployment/retrieval.

7. Follow-up and end-of-day checks

An empty nest is not proof of hatch or predation. Record observed evidence and uncertainty. Continue agreed fate checks through final notA, even if a task disappears. A closed nest can still have a surviving brood; negative broods never receive NESTS closure rows.

Seek broods during ages 21–35 inclusive. From day 36, no qualifying live encounter in that fixed window can suppress brood and associated parent work. A day-25 encounter prevents this particular trigger; a later live encounter can restore work still due after refresh. Renesting and repeated non-brood parent observations have separate, stricter evidence rules. Presumed dead is a planning inference, not an observed death or a reason to enter a D capture. A reliably origin-linked J record resolves chick-stage work under the project rule; it does not prove every sibling survived.

Before finishing:

  • Check that no bird observation, capture, or other required event is recorded only in comments. Save the appropriate event record with truthful details; do not add a duplicate if it is already recorded.
  • Check saved rows against notes, including prefilled dates, observer codes, IDs, GPS pairs, and individual counts. Avoid duplicate submissions.
  • Copy original media, verify the backup, and retain full filenames.
  • Review DATA ENTRY ISSUES. Correct mistakes; explain legitimate exceptions and follow the repair procedure. Never alter truthful evidence to pass a check.
  • Review the refreshed, correctly dated To-Do PDF. Hand over missing photos, unresolved links, contrary live evidence, and unfinished fate checks.
